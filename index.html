What TMA has said, and when

  • Past eventThe 2023/24 El Niño season cost at least 155 lives in Tanzania (reported).
  • 18 August 2026Vuli outlook: above-normal to normal rain in the north and east. Rain starts from the 4th week of September around Lake Victoria and in early October elsewhere, increases in November, and out-of-season rain may continue into January 2027.
  • Early October (reported)Rain already above the monthly average at some stations, including Bukoba and Tabora.
  • 23 October 2026Msimu outlook: above-normal to normal rain in the west, centre and south-west highlands, and normal to above-normal in Ruvuma, Lindi and Mtwara. Rains start from late October to December and end in April to early May, with more rain from November to January than from February to April.

How the rain reaches Tanzania

  1. Warm PacificA very strong El Niño keeps central Pacific waters above average.
  2. Shifted windsMore air rises over the western Indian Ocean and East Africa.
  3. Warm western Indian OceanMoist air flows onto the coast and inland. For Msimu, moist winds from the Congo forests also feed the west and centre.
  4. Local settingLakes, rivers, slopes and drainage decide where rain becomes a flood.

How El Niño affects us

The effects below come from TMA's Vuli advisory of 18 August 2026 and Msimu advisory of 23 October 2026 (both read in full), and from reports by The Chanzo and The Guardian. They are possible in wet areas generally, not certain in any one place.

  • FarmingToo much soil moisture and flooding can damage crops, farm infrastructure and harvesting. Crop diseases of potatoes, sunflower, beans, sesame, fruits and vegetables are expected to increase.
    The good side: the rains can support production.
  • Livestock and fishingWetter conditions may increase Rift Valley fever and foot rot. Seaweed farmers could see disease and lower production because the sea water becomes less salty.
    The good side: pasture and water for animals can improve.
  • HealthContaminated water can cause disease outbreaks, and waterborne and vector-borne diseases are a concern. Landslides are possible around unstable small-scale mining pits.
  • TransportHeavy rain can erode roads, undermine embankments and damage bridges. The Meter Gauge and Standard Gauge Railways, ports and airports can be disrupted.
  • Water and energyRising rivers and reservoirs can threaten water and energy assets and raise water-quality risks, and can cause flooding and sediment build-up.
    The good side: more water may be available.
  • Aviation and portsHeavy rain, lightning and strong winds can reduce visibility and delay or cancel flights, and water transport will be affected. Cargo that must stay dry can be delayed at ports.
  • Local governmentLocal authorities carry much of the response: drains, evacuation routes and early warnings. See "What to do" below.

The Msimu advisory adds that moist westerly winds from the Congo forests should strengthen the rain in the west and centre. Also mentioned: delays and higher costs on building sites, and wildlife moving into neighbouring communities when rain is heavy.

What to do

  • HouseholdsFollow official forecasts and alerts, and do not wait for the heavy rains to begin. Clear drainage channels, keep important documents safe, know a safer route, and agree where children should go. Never cross moving water, and keep children away from swollen streams, culverts and bridges.
    First sentence: TMA. The rest: Daily News editorial, 10 September 2026.
  • FarmersUse weather information. Prepare fields and plant on time. Improve drainage, prevent soil erosion and standing water, and use suitable seeds and inputs.
    Source: TMA Vuli and Msimu advisories.
  • Local governmentsClear and improve drainage. Strengthen village and district disaster committees. Issue early warnings and teach communities. Inspect infrastructure and keep evacuation routes marked.
    Source: TMA advisories, with the last point from the Daily News editorial.
  • Health servicesKeep contingency plans and stock medicines and supplies. Prepare for roads being cut. Remove mosquito breeding sites, and keep services running.
    Source: TMA Vuli and Msimu advisories.
  • Builders, transport, water and energyContractors should move heavy machinery out of valleys and low, waterlogged ground before the rains. Transport agencies should inspect weak points, and water and energy authorities should manage rising water levels.
    Source: TMA Vuli and Msimu advisories.
  • Where to get updatesTMA issues seasonal, monthly, 10-day, daily and special warnings (meteo.go.tz). The Prime Minister's Office Emergency Operations and Communication Centre sends alerts by SMS, media and digital channels. Local forecasts now cover 86 districts in the Vuli regions.
    Source: The Chanzo, 19 August 2026.
